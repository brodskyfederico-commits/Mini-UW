Qué usa IA. Cinco pasos, y solo para leer o redactar: la clasificación de documentos del
paso 1, la extracción del paso 3, la búsqueda oficial del paso 4, la redacción del paso 9 y la
relectura del auditor en el paso 10. Todo número, escenario y veredicto sale de código.
Flujo de información. Cuatro fuentes alimentan el motor, cada una con una sola etiqueta:
el Input Sheet (INPUT), los documentos del deal (DOCUMENTED, con cita), las fuentes
oficiales (OFFICIAL, con link) y las reglas de la casa (HOUSE, en config.yaml ). Se juntan
en el paquete cerrado del paso 5, y el motor no lee nada fuera de ese paquete. El NOI
estabilizado alimenta el refinanciamiento y la venta; el NOI del lease-up entra al flujo
mensual.

=== PAGE 15 ===
1. La IA nunca calcula. Clasifica documentos, extrae datos con su cita, busca las fuentes
oficiales y redacta textos. El pro forma, los escenarios, el scorecard y el veredicto los
hace código.
2. Vacío no es cero. Cada campo tiene uno de cinco estados: OK, CERO EXPLÍCITO,
FALTA, TEXTO INVÁLIDO o FUERA DE RANGO. Ningún vacío se convierte en 0, y
cualquier resultado que dependa de un dato que falta sale como "n/a".
3. Todo número lleva etiqueta y fuente. Las etiquetas son INPUT, DOCUMENTED,
OFFICIAL, HOUSE, CONDITIONAL y UNDERWRITING ASSUMPTION. Un número sin
etiqueta bloquea la página.
4. Las reglas de la casa son configuración, no texto. Umbrales, escenarios y políticas
viven en config.yaml , versionado. Cambiarlos no requiere tocar el código.
5. Una sola pregunta. Si falta algo, el sistema reúne todos los faltantes en una sola tabla y
se detiene. Nunca pregunta de a uno.
6. Determinismo. Los mismos inputs dan siempre el mismo resultado. Cada corrida
guarda los inputs, la versión de las reglas y la versión del motor.
7. Un auditor independiente. Un agente distinto al que armó la página verifica las
etiquetas y que cada cifra coincida con los resultados.
Procedimiento paso a paso
Cada paso recibe la salida del anterior. Los controles 1 a 4 son los únicos puntos donde el
proceso puede detenerse o quedar en HOLD.

Bots y herramientas
El Mini-UW usa 16 bots por deal: 7 con IA y 9 programas sin IA, más un bot de pruebas que
no corre en los deals. Cada dato que entra lo leen dos bots por caminos distintos, y ningún
bot revisa su propio trabajo. Las herramientas externas solo leen valores que ya están
escritos en los documentos; ninguna mide planos, porque el prompt prohíbe el takeoff.
Equipo de bots
Cuatro carriles corren al mismo tiempo y se juntan en el guardián: el Input Sheet (bot 4),
los planos (bots 5 a 7), los documentos financieros (bots 8 y 9) y los datos oficiales (bots
10 y 11). Nada pasa al motor hasta que el guardián confirma que no falta ningún dato.
Después siguen el motor (Control 2), el juez (Control 3), el redactor y el auditor (Control
4), y la revisión de Lorena.
Los 16 bots
La columna "No puede" es la que vuelve al sistema a prueba de errores: cada bot tiene
prohibido justo lo que podría contaminar el resultado.

Procedimiento de cada bot
Cada bot entrega un archivo JSON con un esquema fijo. Si el archivo no cumple el
esquema, el orquestador lo rechaza y no sigue.
Entrada: bots 1 a 4
1. El orquestador abre una corrida con un número único y registra la versión del prompt,
de la configuración y del motor.
2. Recepción lista los archivos y guarda la huella (hash) de cada uno. Si un archivo cambia,
la corrida queda vieja.
3. El clasificador asigna un tipo a cada documento. Si un documento no se puede
clasificar, queda como "sin tipo" y se pregunta qué es.
4. El validador lee el Input Sheet celda por celda. Cada celda sale con su valor (o sin
valor), su estado y la fuente que escribió Underwriten en la columna F.
Planos: bots 5 a 7
1. Para cada dato de la sección 2 del prompt, el lector A busca la tabla o el texto que lo
dice explícitamente y anota la hoja, la tabla y la fila.
2. El lector B hace lo mismo con la imagen de la hoja, sin acceso al resultado de A.
3. Ninguno de los dos mide, cuenta tags ni reconstruye áreas. Si un dato no está escrito, lo
informa como "no está".
4. El conciliador compara los dos resultados. Si coinciden, el dato pasa como
DOCUMENTED con ambas citas. Si difieren, o si un plano contradice a la narrativa, va a
la tabla de conflictos con los dos valores.
Documentos financieros: bots 8 y 9
1. El lector financiero extrae del LOI, el presupuesto del GC y el term sheet solo los
valores que tienen un campo en el Input Sheet, con su página.
2. Del presupuesto del GC extrae además el tipo de contrato, las allowances, las
exclusiones y la contingencia del contratista.
3. El control cruzado compara cada valor con su celda. Si hay diferencia, la pregunta
muestra ambos valores. Ejemplos: una exclusión del GC que no está en soft costs, una
contingencia que aparece dos veces, un LTC del term sheet distinto al de C51.

1. El guardián junta los faltantes y conflictos de todos los carriles y emite una sola tabla.
Si la tabla tiene filas, la corrida se detiene.
2. Con el paquete cerrado, el motor corre el Base y los escenarios y verifica que todo
cierre (Control 2).
3. El juez compara las métricas con los umbrales de config.yaml  y aplica el orden del
veredicto.
Salida: bots 15 y 16
1. El redactor escribe los textos con marcadores (por ejemplo, {yoc_base} ); el
maquetador los reemplaza con las cifras del archivo de resultados y arma la página.
2. El auditor recibe solo la página y los resultados. Verifica que cada número tenga
etiqueta y que cada cifra coincida.
3. Si encuentra una diferencia, la corrida vuelve al bot 15. Después del máximo de vueltas,
pasa a una persona. Si no hay ninguna diferencia, pasa a revisión de Lorena.
Cómo se hace eficiente
Son seis medidas, y ninguna afloja un control.
1. Cuatro carriles en paralelo. Input Sheet (bot 4), planos (bots 5 a 7), documentos
financieros (bots 8 y 9) y datos oficiales (bots 10 y 11) corren al mismo tiempo y solo se
esperan en el guardián.
2. Una sola ronda de preguntas. El guardián junta todos los faltantes y conflictos en una
tabla, en lugar de preguntar carril por carril.
3. Re-corridas parciales. Cuando Underwriten responde, solo se vuelve a correr lo que
depende del dato nuevo. La huella de cada archivo indica qué cambió.

Especificación técnica de los bots
Cada bot es un módulo de Python con una sola tarea, recibe y entrega archivos JSON con
un esquema fijo, y deja todo guardado por corrida. Los bots con IA solo clasifican, leen y
redactan; los números los calculan programas determinísticos.
Base común
Lenguaje: Python 3. Cada bot es un módulo con una función de entrada y una de salida.
Contratos de datos: cada archivo JSON tiene un esquema validado (pydantic). Si un
archivo no cumple su esquema, el orquestador lo rechaza y la corrida se detiene.
Carpeta por corrida: runs/{run_id}/ , con una subcarpeta por bot. Nada se
sobrescribe: una corrección genera una corrida nueva.
Huella de cada archivo: SHA-256 de cada documento recibido. Si un archivo cambia,
solo se vuelve a correr lo que depende de él.
Configuración versionada: config.yaml , con umbrales, escenarios, las preguntas 1 a
14 (sin valor por defecto), las preguntas 15 a 20 (con la opción A cargada), rangos de
validación, reglas del control cruzado y convenciones de cálculo. Cada cambio guarda
quién lo autorizó.
Vacío no es cero en el código: un valor faltante se representa como null  con su
estado, nunca como 0 . Cualquier operación con un null  da null , y el resultado sale
como "n/a".
Estados de celda: OK, CERO EXPLÍCITO, FALTA, TEXTO INVÁLIDO y FUERA DE
RANGO, iguales en el bot 4, en inputs.json  y en la columna de estado del Input
Sheet.
Bots con IA: siempre con salida en JSON con esquema fijo y cita obligatoria por dato.
Una respuesta sin cita se descarta.
Caché oficial: cache/official/{publicador}/{año}/ , con la copia del documento
fuente, su link y la fecha de descarga.
Confidencialidad: los documentos del deal que se envían a servicios en la nube
(Document Intelligence y el modelo de visión) siguen la política de retención y acceso
que defina el equipo, compatible con los NDA de cada deal.

17. Verdict Rules Policy: HOLD – MORE DD REQUIRED: Material information required to
support the investment conclusion is missing, unresolved or materially uncertain. This
classification overrides an economic PASS when the missing information could materially
affect cost, revenue, financing, valuation or equity returns. FAIL – DO NOT PURSUE: Any
applicable Base Case metric breaches its defined FAIL threshold, or the project cannot
support the required economics at the current purchase price / JV structure. The Mini-
UW must still identify the Maximum Supportable Purchase Price or the economic
condition required to make the project potentially investable. PASS – PROCEED TO FULL
UW: Every applicable Base and Downside metric meets its PASS threshold, no material DD
deficiency capable of changing the conclusion remains unresolved, and the project does
not depend on Upside assumptions to pass. HOLD – ECONOMICS NOT SUPPORTED: The
project does not breach a FAIL threshold but one or more applicable Base or Downside
PASS thresholds are not met. The Mini-UW must identify the principal economic
deficiency and the purchase price, cost, revenue, financing or structural condition
required for the project to pass. General rule: Only metrics applicable to the project
strategy may trigger PASS / HOLD / FAIL. A metric that is not applicable to the strategy
must be treated as N/A and must not cause an automatic failure. Upside assumptions may
never rescue a weak Base Case.

3. Downside Case Policy: Mini-UW must run both one-driver-at-a-time sensitivities and
an integrated downside case. Single-driver sensitivities identify the variables to which the
project is most exposed; the integrated downside case determines whether the
investment remains supportable under simultaneous adverse assumptions.
4. GBA Policy: When the current plans show GBA as ‘TBD,’ Mini-UW may use a plan-
derived working GBA from an auditable area takeoff, clearly flagged as PLAN-DERIVED /
UNDERWRITING ASSUMPTION. The source sheets, takeoff method and reconciliation
must be retained. If the area cannot be measured reliably or the uncertainty could
materially change TDC or investment economics, the project must remain HOLD – MORE
DD REQUIRED.
5. Tax Exemption Policy: A tax exemption or statutory incentive may be included in the
Mini-UW Base Case only when there is credible project-specific evidence that the project
is reasonably expected to qualify. If the benefit is not fully documented, it must be clearly
flagged as CONDITIONAL / POTENTIAL and the Downside Case must remove or
materially reduce the benefit. No project may receive a clean PASS if the economics
depend materially on an undocumented exemption.
6. Property Tax Policy: For new construction, Mini-UW must estimate stabilized property
taxes using both an income/value-based proxy and current assessor/comparable
evidence where available. The Base Case should use the higher supportable tax estimate
unless a documented statutory exemption or project-specific assessment treatment
justifies a different result. Any material uncertainty must be flagged and sensitized in
Downside.
7. Yield on Cost Policy: Yield on Cost is calculated as stabilized NOI divided by true all-in
TDC. The house thresholds are default screening gates for ground-up multifamily and
mixed-use projects and must be evaluated together with the development spread over
market cap rate. A project cannot receive a clean PASS solely because it meets the
absolute Yield on Cost threshold if the spread to market cap is insufficient.
8. Development Spread Policy: Development spread is calculated as stabilized Yield on
Cost minus the applicable market capitalization rate. The Base Case should maintain a
minimum positive spread sufficient to compensate for development, lease-up, financing
and execution risk. A project with a Base Case spread at or below 0 bps cannot receive a
PASS, regardless of absolute Yield on Cost.

9. Equity Recovery at Refinance Policy: Equity recovered at refinance is calculated as net
cash-out available to equity after construction/bridge debt payoff, refinance costs,
required reserves and other debt, divided by total equity invested through stabilization.
The metric applies only to strategies where refinance is part of the business plan. A clean
PASS requires material capital recycling without relying on unsupported appraisal
leverage.
10. Equity Cash-In at Refinance Policy: Equity cash-in at refinance is calculated as
additional equity required to complete the refinance after applying permanent-loan
proceeds against construction/bridge debt payoff, refinance costs, required reserves and
other debt, divided by total equity invested through stabilization. The metric applies only
when refinance is part of the business plan. A clean PASS should not depend on a material
second equity injection at stabilization.
11. Break-Even Occupancy Policy: Break-even occupancy is the minimum stabilized
occupancy required for effective revenue to cover operating expenses and required debt
service without additional equity support. Lower is better. A clean PASS requires
sufficient occupancy cushion to absorb normal vacancy, concessions and operating
volatility; projects with Base Case break-even occupancy above 93% are considered
structurally fragile.
12. Post-Refi Cash-on-Cash Policy: Post-refi cash-on-cash is calculated as annual cash
flow available to equity after permanent debt service divided by the equity capital still
invested after refinance. The metric applies only to hold strategies where equity remains
invested after refinance. A clean PASS requires that the residual equity continue to
generate an acceptable current return and not rely solely on future appreciation.
13. Asking Price vs. Residual Land Value Policy: Residual Land Value is the maximum
land/purchase basis supported by project economics after satisfying required
development, financing and equity-return thresholds. Asking price is a commercial
reference only. A clean PASS requires asking price at or below Base Case RLV. If asking
price exceeds Base Case RLV by more than 15%, the deal is classified as FAIL – DO NOT
PURSUE at the current asking price, while the Mini-UW must still report the maximum
supportable purchase price.
14. Equity-to-TDC Policy: Equity-to-TDC is calculated as total equity required through
stabilization divided by true all-in TDC. The metric is used to test capital efficiency and
financing support. For Underwriten, a preferred equity requirement is generally in the
20%–25% range when supported by market debt and sustainable refinance economics;
however, lower equity does not automatically improve the underwriting classification
because it may simply reflect excessive leverage. A clean PASS should generally require

15. Levered IRR Policy: Levered IRR is calculated on project-level equity cash flows using
the actual timing of equity contributions, operating distributions, refinance proceeds and
exit or terminal value assumptions applicable to the strategy. The Base Case minimum is
15% and the Downside minimum is 12%. A Base Case below 10% is considered insufficient
for ground-up development risk. Levered IRR must not be improved through unsupported
leverage, optimistic refinance proceeds, aggressive exit valuation or delayed recognition
of equity requirements. IRR must be evaluated together with Equity Multiple, equity
recovery, Equity-to-TDC and Downside performance.
16. Equity Multiple Policy: Equity Multiple is calculated as total cash distributions and
realized equity proceeds divided by total equity invested. For Mini-UW screening, the
default measurement horizon is five years from stabilization unless a different hold period
is explicitly documented for the strategy. The Base Case minimum is 1.75x and the
Downside minimum is 1.50x. A Base Case below 1.35x is considered insufficient for
ground-up development risk. Equity Multiple must be evaluated together with Levered
IRR because Equity Multiple does not reflect the timing of cash flows.
17. Verdict Rules Policy: HOLD – MORE DD REQUIRED: Material information required to
support the investment conclusion is missing, unresolved or materially uncertain. This
classification overrides an economic PASS when the missing information could materially
affect cost, revenue, financing, valuation or equity returns. FAIL – DO NOT PURSUE: Any
applicable Base Case metric breaches its defined FAIL threshold, or the project cannot
support the required economics at the current purchase price / JV structure. The Mini-
UW must still identify the Maximum Supportable Purchase Price or the economic
condition required to make the project potentially investable. PASS – PROCEED TO FULL
UW: Every applicable Base and Downside metric meets its PASS threshold, no material DD
deficiency capable of changing the conclusion remains unresolved, and the project does
not depend on Upside assumptions to pass. HOLD – ECONOMICS NOT SUPPORTED: The
project does not breach a FAIL threshold but one or more applicable Base or Downside
PASS thresholds are not met. The Mini-UW must identify the principal economic
deficiency and the purchase price, cost, revenue, financing or structural condition

18. Residential Rent Sensitivity Policy: Residential rent sensitivities are applied to
achievable market rents, not seller or developer asking rents. The standard Mini-UW
stress is -10% in Downside and +5% in Upside unless stronger project-specific market
evidence supports a different range. Upside rents may never be used to rescue a weak
Base Case.
19. Commercial Rent Sensitivity Policy: Commercial rent sensitivities are applied to
achievable market rents supported by current comparable evidence, not broker asking
rents or unsupported pro forma assumptions. The standard Mini-UW stress is -10% in
Downside and +5% in Upside unless project-specific leasing conditions, tenant quality,
absorption risk or market evidence justify a different range. For mixed-use projects,
commercial rent risk must be evaluated separately from residential rent risk. Upside
commercial rent assumptions may never be used to rescue a weak Base Case.
20. Residential Vacancy Sensitivity Policy: Residential vacancy sensitivities are applied
as percentage-point changes to the Base Case stabilized vacancy assumption, not as
percentage changes to vacancy itself. The standard Mini-UW stress is +3.0 percentage
points in Downside and -1.0 percentage point in Upside unless project-specific market
evidence supports a different range. Vacancy assumptions must reflect achievable
stabilized occupancy, concessions and lease-up conditions. Upside vacancy assumptions
may never be used to rescue a weak Base Case.
21. Commercial Vacancy Sensitivity Policy: Commercial vacancy sensitivities are applied
as percentage-point changes to the Base Case stabilized commercial vacancy
assumption. The standard Mini-UW stress is +5.0 percentage points in Downside and -2.0
percentage points in Upside unless project-specific lease, tenant, market or absorption
evidence supports a different range. Commercial vacancy must be evaluated separately
from residential vacancy and must reflect realistic lease-up and tenant-retention risk.
22. Hard Cost Sensitivity Policy: Hard construction, sitework and parking costs are
stressed together for Mini-UW screening unless a more detailed cost breakdown
supports separate sensitivities. The standard stress is +10% in Downside and -5% in
Upside. Cost reductions in Upside must not rely on unsupported value engineering,
omitted scope or exclusions required to deliver the completed project.

23. Construction Period Sensitivity Policy: The standard Mini-UW construction-period
stress is +6 months in Downside and -3 months in Upside. The change must flow through
interest carry, taxes, insurance, general conditions, financing costs and other time-
dependent development costs where applicable. An accelerated schedule may be used in
Upside only when operationally supportable.
24. Lease-Up Period Sensitivity Policy: The standard Mini-UW lease-up stress is +6
months in Downside and -3 months in Upside. The sensitivity must affect the timing of
stabilization, operating cash flow, interest carry, refinance timing and equity recovery
where applicable. Lease-up assumptions must be supported by achievable absorption
rather than developer targets.
25. Construction Loan Rate Sensitivity Policy: The standard Mini-UW construction-loan-
rate stress is +150 bps in Downside and -50 bps in Upside relative to the Base Case rate.
Interest expense must be calculated using the applicable construction draw profile and
development timing. The model must not reduce equity requirements through
unsupported construction leverage.
26. Permanent Loan Rate Sensitivity Policy: The standard Mini-UW permanent-loan-rate
stress is +100 bps in Downside and -50 bps in Upside relative to the Base Case rate. The
permanent loan must always be constrained by the lower of the LTV-supported loan and
the DSCR-supported loan. Lower interest rates in Upside may not be used to override
leverage or debt-service constraints.
27. Refinance Cap Rate Sensitivity Policy: The standard Mini-UW refinance
capitalization-rate stress is +50 bps in Downside and -25 bps in Upside relative to the
Base Case market cap rate. Stabilized value must be calculated from supportable NOI and
a market-supported capitalization rate. Cap-rate compression in Upside must not be used
to rescue a weak Base Case.
28. Insurance Sensitivity Policy: The standard Mini-UW insurance stress is +20% in
Downside and -10% in Upside relative to the Base Case annual insurance expense. For
markets with elevated catastrophe, coastal, flood, wind or replacement-cost exposure, a
more severe Downside assumption may be required based on project-specific evidence.
29. Property Tax Sensitivity Policy: The standard Mini-UW property-tax stress is +15% in
Downside and -10% in Upside relative to the Base Case stabilized property-tax expense.
Any statutory exemption, abatement or incentive must follow the Tax Exemption Policy.
The Downside Case must reduce or eliminate benefits that are conditional,
undocumented or exposed to qualification risk.

30. Permanent LTV Sensitivity Policy: The standard Mini-UW permanent-loan maximum-
LTV stress is -5.0 percentage points in Downside and +5.0 percentage points in Upside
relative to the Base Case maximum LTV. Permanent loan proceeds must always equal the
lower of the LTV-constrained loan and the DSCR-constrained loan. Higher LTV in Upside
may not exceed credible market financing terms and may never be used to rescue a weak
Base Case.
31. Exit Cap Rate Sensitivity Policy: When a terminal sale or exit value is included in the
Mini-UW, the standard exit-cap-rate stress is +50 bps in Downside and -25 bps in Upside
relative to the Base Case exit cap rate. Exit valuation must be based on supportable
forward NOI and a defensible market capitalization rate. Cap-rate compression may not
be assumed solely to achieve the required IRR or Equity Multiple.
32. Rent Growth Sensitivity Policy: When rent growth is included in the project-level
cash-flow model, the standard Mini-UW stress is -1.0 percentage point per year in
Downside and +0.5 percentage point per year in Upside relative to the Base Case annual
rent-growth assumption. Rent growth must remain consistent with market evidence and
the applicable hold period. Upside rent growth may not be used to rescue a weak Base
Case.
33. Scenario Override and Application Policy: The Section 3 assumptions are
Underwriten default screening sensitivities and serve as fallback assumptions when
stronger project-specific evidence is not available. Project-specific market, construction,
financing, operating or legal evidence may justify a different sensitivity, but every
override must be explicitly identified as an UNDERWRITING ASSUMPTION, state its
source and explain why it differs from the Underwriten default. The integrated Downside
Case must apply all applicable Downside drivers simultaneously. One-driver-at-a-time
sensitivities must also be reported separately to identify the variables with the greatest
impact on project economics. Upside assumptions are informational only and may never
convert a project that fails Base Case requirements into PASS – PROCEED TO FULL UW.
