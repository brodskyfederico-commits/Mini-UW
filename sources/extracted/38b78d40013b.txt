[paragraph 132] 4. Comments   Optional.LP waterfall terms (if LP), edits to the verdict rules, and any house defaults you use (operating costs per unit, soft-cost %, contingency, debt terms):1. IRR / Equity Multiple Policy:Mini-UW must calculate project-level Equity Multiple and Levered IRR using a simplified but auditable cash-flow model. Full UW may later refine timing, waterfall, promote and investor-specific distributions.2. Return Level Policy:Mini-UW return metrics are calculated at project level on total equity invested. LP-specific returns after fees, promote, preferred return or waterfall are calculated only when the applicable JV/equity structure is documented and are shown separately from project-level economics.3. Downside Case Policy:Mini-UW must run both one-driver-at-a-time sensitivities and an integrated downside case. Single-driver sensitivities identify the variables to which the project is most exposed; the integrated downside case determines whether the investment remains supportable under simultaneous adverse assumptions.4. GBA Policy:When the current plans show GBA as ‘TBD,’ Mini-UW may use a plan-derived working GBA from an auditable area takeoff, clearly flagged as PLAN-DERIVED / UNDERWRITING ASSUMPTION. The source sheets, takeoff method and reconciliation must be retained. If the area cannot be measured reliably or the uncertainty could materially change TDC or investment economics, the project must remain HOLD – MORE DD REQUIRED.5. Tax Exemption Policy:A tax exemption or statutory incentive may be included in the Mini-UW Base Case only when there is credible project-specific evidence that the project is reasonably expected to qualify. If the benefit is not fully documented, it must be clearly flagged as CONDITIONAL / POTENTIAL and the Downside Case must remove or materially reduce the benefit. No project may receive a clean PASS if the economics depend materially on an undocumented exemption.6. Property Tax Policy:For new construction, Mini-UW must estimate stabilized property taxes using both an income/value-based proxy and current assessor/comparable evidence where available. The Base Case should use the higher supportable tax estimate unless a documented statutory exemption or project-specific assessment treatment justifies a different result. Any material uncertainty must be flagged and sensitized in Downside.7. Yield on Cost Policy:Yield on Cost is calculated as stabilized NOI divided by true all-in TDC. The house thresholds are default screening gates for ground-up multifamily and mixed-use projects and must be evaluated together with the development spread over market cap rate. A project cannot receive a clean PASS solely because it meets the absolute Yield on Cost threshold if the spread to market cap is insufficient.8. Development Spread Policy:Development spread is calculated as stabilized Yield on Cost minus the applicable market capitalization rate. The Base Case should maintain a minimum positive spread sufficient to compensate for development, lease-up, financing and execution risk. A project with a Base Case spread at or below 0 bps cannot receive a PASS, regardless of absolute Yield on Cost.9. Equity Recovery at Refinance Policy:Equity recovered at refinance is calculated as net cash-out available to equity after construction/bridge debt payoff, refinance costs, required reserves and other debt, divided by total equity invested through stabilization. The metric applies only to strategies where refinance is part of the business plan. A clean PASS requires material capital recycling without relying on unsupported appraisal leverage.10. Equity Cash-In at Refinance Policy:Equity cash-in at refinance is calculated as additional equity required to complete the refinance after applying permanent-loan proceeds against construction/bridge debt payoff, refinance costs, required reserves and other debt, divided by total equity invested through stabilization. The metric applies only when refinance is part of the business plan. A clean PASS should not depend on a material second equity injection at stabilization.11. Break-Even Occupancy Policy:Break-even occupancy is the minimum stabilized occupancy required for effective revenue to cover operating expenses and required debt service without additional equity support. Lower is better. A clean PASS requires sufficient occupancy cushion to absorb normal vacancy, concessions and operating volatility; projects with Base Case break-even occupancy above 93% are considered structurally fragile.12. Post-Refi Cash-on-Cash Policy:Post-refi cash-on-cash is calculated as annual cash flow available to equity after permanent debt service divided by the equity capital still invested after refinance. The metric applies only to hold strategies where equity remains invested after refinance. A clean PASS requires that the residual equity continue to generate an acceptable current return and not rely solely on future appreciation.13. Asking Price vs. Residual Land Value Policy:Residual Land Value is the maximum land/purchase basis supported by project economics after satisfying required development, financing and equity-return thresholds. Asking price is a commercial reference only. A clean PASS requires asking price at or below Base Case RLV. If asking price exceeds Base Case RLV by more than 15%, the deal is classified as FAIL – DO NOT PURSUE at the current asking price, while the Mini-UW must still report the maximum supportable purchase price.14. Equity-to-TDC Policy:Equity-to-TDC is calculated as total equity required through stabilization divided by true all-in TDC. The metric is used to test capital efficiency and financing support. For Underwriten, a preferred equity requirement is generally in the 20%–25% range when supported by market debt and sustainable refinance economics; however, lower equity does not automatically improve the underwriting classification because it may simply reflect excessive leverage.A clean PASS should generally require Equity-to-TDC at or below 35% in Base and 40% in Downside. A Base Case above 45% is considered economically unattractive for the strategy and should be classified as FAIL – DO NOT PURSUE unless an explicitly documented alternative capitalization structure changes the economics.Equity below 25% of TDC must not be treated as automatically superior. The model must verify that the resulting construction leverage is market-supported and that permanent refinancing remains supportable under both LTV and DSCR.
[paragraph 133] 15. Levered IRR Policy:Levered IRR is calculated on project-level equity cash flows using the actual timing of equity contributions, operating distributions, refinance proceeds and exit or terminal value assumptions applicable to the strategy. The Base Case minimum is 15% and the Downside minimum is 12%. A Base Case below 10% is considered insufficient for ground-up development risk.Levered IRR must not be improved through unsupported leverage, optimistic refinance proceeds, aggressive exit valuation or delayed recognition of equity requirements. IRR must be evaluated together with Equity Multiple, equity recovery, Equity-to-TDC and Downside performance.
[paragraph 134] 16. Equity Multiple Policy:Equity Multiple is calculated as total cash distributions and realized equity proceeds divided by total equity invested. For Mini-UW screening, the default measurement horizon is five years from stabilization unless a different hold period is explicitly documented for the strategy.The Base Case minimum is 1.75x and the Downside minimum is 1.50x. A Base Case below 1.35x is considered insufficient for ground-up development risk.Equity Multiple must be evaluated together with Levered IRR because Equity Multiple does not reflect the timing of cash flows.
[paragraph 135] 17. Verdict Rules Policy:HOLD – MORE DD REQUIRED:Material information required to support the investment conclusion is missing, unresolved or materially uncertain. This classification overrides an economic PASS when the missing information could materially affect cost, revenue, financing, valuation or equity returns.FAIL – DO NOT PURSUE:Any applicable Base Case metric breaches its defined FAIL threshold, or the project cannot support the required economics at the current purchase price / JV structure. The Mini-UW must still identify the Maximum Supportable Purchase Price or the economic condition required to make the project potentially investable.PASS – PROCEED TO FULL UW:Every applicable Base and Downside metric meets its PASS threshold, no material DD deficiency capable of changing the conclusion remains unresolved, and the project does not depend on Upside assumptions to pass.HOLD – ECONOMICS NOT SUPPORTED:The project does not breach a FAIL threshold but one or more applicable Base or Downside PASS thresholds are not met. The Mini-UW must identify the principal economic deficiency and the purchase price, cost, revenue, financing or structural condition required for the project to pass.General rule:Only metrics applicable to the project strategy may trigger PASS / HOLD / FAIL. A metric that is not applicable to the strategy must be treated as N/A and must not cause an automatic failure. Upside assumptions may never rescue a weak Base Case.
[paragraph 136] 18. Residential Rent Sensitivity Policy:Residential rent sensitivities are applied to achievable market rents, not seller or developer asking rents. The standard Mini-UW stress is -10% in Downside and +5% in Upside unless stronger project-specific market evidence supports a different range. Upside rents may never be used to rescue a weak Base Case.
[paragraph 137] 19. Commercial Rent Sensitivity Policy:Commercial rent sensitivities are applied to achievable market rents supported by current comparable evidence, not broker asking rents or unsupported pro forma assumptions. The standard Mini-UW stress is -10% in Downside and +5% in Upside unless project-specific leasing conditions, tenant quality, absorption risk or market evidence justify a different range.For mixed-use projects, commercial rent risk must be evaluated separately from residential rent risk. Upside commercial rent assumptions may never be used to rescue a weak Base Case.
[paragraph 138] 20. Residential Vacancy Sensitivity Policy:Residential vacancy sensitivities are applied as percentage-point changes to the Base Case stabilized vacancy assumption, not as percentage changes to vacancy itself. The standard Mini-UW stress is +3.0 percentage points in Downside and -1.0 percentage point in Upside unless project-specific market evidence supports a different range.Vacancy assumptions must reflect achievable stabilized occupancy, concessions and lease-up conditions. Upside vacancy assumptions may never be used to rescue a weak Base Case.
[paragraph 139] 21. Commercial Vacancy Sensitivity Policy:Commercial vacancy sensitivities are applied as percentage-point changes to the Base Case stabilized commercial vacancy assumption. The standard Mini-UW stress is +5.0 percentage points in Downside and -2.0 percentage points in Upside unless project-specific lease, tenant, market or absorption evidence supports a different range.Commercial vacancy must be evaluated separately from residential vacancy and must reflect realistic lease-up and tenant-retention risk.
[paragraph 140] 22. Hard Cost Sensitivity Policy:Hard construction, sitework and parking costs are stressed together for Mini-UW screening unless a more detailed cost breakdown supports separate sensitivities. The standard stress is +10% in Downside and -5% in Upside.Cost reductions in Upside must not rely on unsupported value engineering, omitted scope or exclusions required to deliver the completed project.
[paragraph 141] 23. Construction Period Sensitivity Policy:The standard Mini-UW construction-period stress is +6 months in Downside and -3 months in Upside. The change must flow through interest carry, taxes, insurance, general conditions, financing costs and other time-dependent development costs where applicable.An accelerated schedule may be used in Upside only when operationally supportable.
[paragraph 142] 24. Lease-Up Period Sensitivity Policy:The standard Mini-UW lease-up stress is +6 months in Downside and -3 months in Upside. The sensitivity must affect the timing of stabilization, operating cash flow, interest carry, refinance timing and equity recovery where applicable.Lease-up assumptions must be supported by achievable absorption rather than developer targets.
[paragraph 143] 25. Construction Loan Rate Sensitivity Policy:The standard Mini-UW construction-loan-rate stress is +150 bps in Downside and -50 bps in Upside relative to the Base Case rate.Interest expense must be calculated using the applicable construction draw profile and development timing. The model must not reduce equity requirements through unsupported construction leverage.
[paragraph 144] 26. Permanent Loan Rate Sensitivity Policy:The standard Mini-UW permanent-loan-rate stress is +100 bps in Downside and -50 bps in Upside relative to the Base Case rate.The permanent loan must always be constrained by the lower of the LTV-supported loan and the DSCR-supported loan. Lower interest rates in Upside may not be used to override leverage or debt-service constraints.
[paragraph 145] 27. Refinance Cap Rate Sensitivity Policy:The standard Mini-UW refinance capitalization-rate stress is +50 bps in Downside and -25 bps in Upside relative to the Base Case market cap rate.Stabilized value must be calculated from supportable NOI and a market-supported capitalization rate. Cap-rate compression in Upside must not be used to rescue a weak Base Case.
[paragraph 146] 28. Insurance Sensitivity Policy:The standard Mini-UW insurance stress is +20% in Downside and -10% in Upside relative to the Base Case annual insurance expense.For markets with elevated catastrophe, coastal, flood, wind or replacement-cost exposure, a more severe Downside assumption may be required based on project-specific evidence.
[paragraph 147] 29. Property Tax Sensitivity Policy:The standard Mini-UW property-tax stress is +15% in Downside and -10% in Upside relative to the Base Case stabilized property-tax expense.Any statutory exemption, abatement or incentive must follow the Tax Exemption Policy. The Downside Case must reduce or eliminate benefits that are conditional, undocumented or exposed to qualification risk.
[paragraph 148] 30. Permanent LTV Sensitivity Policy:The standard Mini-UW permanent-loan maximum-LTV stress is -5.0 percentage points in Downside and +5.0 percentage points in Upside relative to the Base Case maximum LTV.Permanent loan proceeds must always equal the lower of the LTV-constrained loan and the DSCR-constrained loan. Higher LTV in Upside may not exceed credible market financing terms and may never be used to rescue a weak Base Case.
[paragraph 149] 31. Exit Cap Rate Sensitivity Policy:When a terminal sale or exit value is included in the Mini-UW, the standard exit-cap-rate stress is +50 bps in Downside and -25 bps in Upside relative to the Base Case exit cap rate.Exit valuation must be based on supportable forward NOI and a defensible market capitalization rate. Cap-rate compression may not be assumed solely to achieve the required IRR or Equity Multiple.
[paragraph 150] 32. Rent Growth Sensitivity Policy:When rent growth is included in the project-level cash-flow model, the standard Mini-UW stress is -1.0 percentage point per year in Downside and +0.5 percentage point per year in Upside relative to the Base Case annual rent-growth assumption.Rent growth must remain consistent with market evidence and the applicable hold period. Upside rent growth may not be used to rescue a weak Base Case.
[paragraph 151] 33. Scenario Override and Application Policy:The Section 3 assumptions are Underwriten default screening sensitivities and serve as fallback assumptions when stronger project-specific evidence is not available.Project-specific market, construction, financing, operating or legal evidence may justify a different sensitivity, but every override must be explicitly identified as an UNDERWRITING ASSUMPTION, state its source and explain why it differs from the Underwriten default.The integrated Downside Case must apply all applicable Downside drivers simultaneously. One-driver-at-a-time sensitivities must also be reported separately to identify the variables with the greatest impact on project economics.Upside assumptions are informational only and may never convert a project that fails Base Case requirements into PASS – PROCEED TO FULL UW.
